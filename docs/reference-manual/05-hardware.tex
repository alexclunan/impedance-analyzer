\chapter{Hardware and PCB}
\label{ch:hardware}

The instrument is a Xilinx Zynq-7020 system-on-module on a custom mixed-signal
carrier board. This chapter documents the compute module, the board-level signal
chains, and the notable components. Full schematics and the bill of materials are
in \file{PCB/PCB Schematic.pdf} and \file{PCB/PCB\_BoM.csv}; the Altium project
sources are intentionally kept out of the repository.

\section{Compute module (TE0720 SoM)}
The processing core is a \textbf{Trenz TE0720} SoM carrying a Xilinx
\textbf{XC7Z020} Zynq-7000 (dual Cortex-A9 PS + Artix-7-class PL), DDR3, and boot
flash. The PS runs PetaLinux; the PL holds the DSP pipeline. The SoM plugs into
the custom carrier through the FMC-style connector (see the schematic's
\textbf{TE0701} and \textbf{VITA} sheets). See the TE0720 TRM in
\file{Reference/} for module pinout and power sequencing.

\section{Signal chains}
The schematic is organized into sheets, each a signal chain. The parts below are
from the BOM.

\subsection{Excitation (DAC sheet)}
The PL synthesizes a composite multi-tone waveform (Chapter~\ref{ch:signal}) and
drives the \textbf{AD9122} DAC. The DAC's differential output is reconstructed
and buffered by a wideband amplifier (\textbf{ADA4857-1YRZ}, 850\,MHz) and
brought out on an SMA edge connector to the sensor electrodes.

\subsection{Acquisition (ADC sheet)}
The returning sensor current enters on an SMA connector and is converted to a
voltage and conditioned by a low-noise front end:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{ADA4817-1ACPZ} ($\times$2) --- 1.05\,GHz, low-noise FET-input
        amplifier(s): the transimpedance / front-end stage.
  \item \textbf{LMH6881SQ} --- 2.4\,GHz \emph{programmable-gain} differential
        amplifier. This is the \textbf{PGA} whose gain is set by
        \reg{ANALOG1[3:0]} (gain $= 26 - 2\cdot\text{code}$\,dB).
  \item \textbf{ADA4938-1ACPZ} --- differential ADC driver into the AD9467.
\end{itemize}
The AD9467's 16-bit LVDS output is captured in the PL
(\code{adc\_lvds\_capture}) with delay-matched traces.

\subsection{Clocking (Clocking sheet)}
A \textbf{200\,MHz LVDS oscillator} (\textbf{AK2ADDF1-200.0000T}) is the master
timebase. A \textbf{LMK00301SQE} 3-GHz differential fan-out buffer / level
translator distributes it to the ADC, DAC, and PL. The single shared clock is
why the PS, PL, and the record timestamps all share one time base with no clock
synchronization (Chapter~\ref{ch:arch}).

\subsection{Power (Power sheet)}
A low-noise rail tree suited to the data converters: \textbf{LTM8074IY} ($\times$2)
$\mu$Module buck regulators for the bulk rails, followed by ultra-low-noise LDOs
--- \textbf{LT3045IDD} ($\times$3, positive), \textbf{TPS7A9401DSCR} ($\times$2),
and \textbf{LT3094HMSE} (negative) --- for the sensitive analog supplies. Input
protection uses an \textbf{STPS2L30A} Schottky, an \textbf{SMBJ14A} TVS, and
\textbf{ESD8011}/\textbf{ESD9B} ESD diodes.

\subsection{Interconnect}
\textbf{ASP-134604-01} --- male FMC connector (1.27\,mm, 4$\times$40) to the SoM/
carrier (VITA/TE0701 sheets). Three \textbf{0732511150} SMA edge connectors carry
the analog excitation output and sensor input.

\section{Component summary}

amplifiers, 1 programmable-gain amplifier, 1 clock oscillator, 1 clock fan-out

capacitors / 52 resistors / 4 inductors / 5 ferrite beads.
